Before work begins

Name the approver and the person who opens the door.

Households can outgrow their wiring. In a busy family home with more people and devices than it was first wired for, kitchen, laundry and bedroom circuits may carry loads nobody planned, and several adults may each assume another will handle the contractor.

Write down who owns the property, who can sign for changes and who will be home to open the door on the day. Agree in writing which person decides when household members disagree.

Who should hold the paperwork once the work is finished?

Agree before work starts which adult keeps the written scope, the ESA notification details and the contractor's invoice. Keep them together where every adult in the household can find them, and photograph the panel door labels afterwards.

Should a charger and a panel upgrade be approved together?

They can be separate decisions, but the contractor should explain how one affects the other. Ask for the proposed charger load to be stated next to the existing household loads in one written scope.

How do I compare quotes when two adults want different things?

Have each contractor price the same written scope, listing what is included and what is excluded. Then the adults can compare like with like and decide together, rather than weighing different assumptions.

What if nobody is home when the contractor needs to get in?

Do not leave the arrangement vague. Name one adult who can admit the contractor, confirm that person in writing and agree what happens if the visit must be moved. Ask whether any area needs an adult present throughout.

Does sending the form mean someone is coming to the door?

Sending the form brings no visit. This site of guides receives and reviews the enquiry, without pricing or doing electrical work. Later, if you deal with a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor, it gives you its name and the job's scope; check its licence through ESA, then agree cost and dates.
