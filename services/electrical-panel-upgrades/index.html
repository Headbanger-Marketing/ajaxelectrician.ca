A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Ajax property

List what the sub-panel would serve, from a workshop bench to a basement laundry, and who in the household uses each space. Photograph the panel door and its labels from outside, without opening anything.

Ask who may approve work in an addition or detached space, and who must be present to unlock the garage or basement. Ask the contractor to state where the new board would be reached.

What should the assessment establish?

An Ajax household wants a sub-panel to serve a garage, basement or addition. What that new board will feed is established, and so is whether the present supply has room for that demand on top of everything the family already runs. Where the board will sit is named, with who can reach it and any space that has to stay clear. The contractor explains, in plain terms, how the main panel will feed it. The written scope then records who approved the work, who opens the door, whether the utility must be involved and how the new board will be labelled.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a garage sub-panel be approved by one adult alone?

That depends on who owns the property and who can sign for changes, so confirm it in writing rather than assuming. The contractor will still need the person who holds that authority to approve the written scope.

Does a basement sub-panel mean the main panel is replaced?

Not necessarily. A sub-panel adds a distribution point fed from existing equipment, while replacing the main panel is a different job. Ask the contractor which one is proposed and why, and have that written into the scope.

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
