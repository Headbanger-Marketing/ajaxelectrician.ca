EV charger installation in Ajax.

A charger planned for assigned or shared parking at an Ajax property needs permission as well as a circuit. The person who owns the space is not always the person who lives beside it.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Ajax property

Note the exact parking position, who is assigned to it, which household drivers will use it and how far it sits from the panel or meter. Collect any written rules, agreements or letters that mention parking, wiring or shared areas.

Ask who must approve a charger on a space that is not solely yours, and whether the cable route crosses shared property. Ask the contractor to record each approval in the written scope.

What should the assessment establish?

The scope names who may approve a charger on an assigned or shared Ajax space and what that approval covers. A layout of the installation shows where the equipment mounts, how the circuit reaches it and whether the route crosses property you do not control. A load comparison, written so the household can follow it, sets the proposed charger load beside the loads already running and gives the contractor's view on whether the supply suits it. A brief line names who can open any gate or door, and another says who removes the equipment later.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I install a charger on a space I only have the use of?

Possibly, but only with the permission of whoever controls that space, and the contractor should see it in writing. Ask what the approval allows, including the cable route and where the equipment will be fixed.

Does a shared parking charger need its own circuit?

ESA advises a dedicated circuit for approved charging equipment. Whether shared parking changes the design is for the contractor to assess, so ask how metering, access and the existing supply were considered.

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
